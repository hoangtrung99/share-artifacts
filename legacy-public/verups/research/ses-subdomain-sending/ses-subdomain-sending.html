2 Which SES configuration actually authorizes the From address

Every email carries two source addresses. The From header is what recipients see and is what DKIM/DMARC judge. The MAIL FROM (envelope / Return-Path) is where bounces go and is what SPF judges. SES configures these through distinct surfaces — only one of them is "send from the subdomain".

The single most important finding: the stg-ex.backseat-service.com hosted zone you manage (VerUp-STG 520411743393) is genuinely authoritative. The parent delegates the subdomain to exactly the four name servers your zone uses, and the subdomain's apex record already resolves publicly. Any DKIM record you add to this zone will be served.

Why this matters — it is the opposite of a wrong-zone trap

The risk with this kind of setup is publishing DKIM records into a hosted zone that the registrar does not delegate to — records that are never served. Here the proof rules that out: the public NS for stg-ex.backseat-service.com matches your zone's name servers, and the apex A record resolves. The delegation chain is intact, so verifying the subdomain is a self-contained, low-risk action inside an account you control.

The principled reason B fits, not just "cleaner"

The stg-ex subdomain was deliberately delegated into the VerUp-STG account — a per-environment isolation boundary. Verifying the identity at that same boundary keeps DNS ownership, the SES identity, and the sending application all inside one account. Independent of taste, B removes a cross-account/cross-zone dependency that A introduces.

5 DMARC alignment — why both pass today, why B is robust

DMARC passes when the From domain aligns with the DKIM d= domain (or with the SPF/MAIL FROM domain). "Relaxed" alignment means the two share an organizational domain; "strict" means they must be identical. Your parent policy is p=none with no adkim tag, so alignment defaults to relaxed.

Reading the AWS wording correctly

The SES guide says "if sending from a subdomain of the domain in the From address, ensure that your DMARC policy is set to relaxed alignment", and elsewhere notes SES signs with the exact identity domain ("strict DKIM by default"). These are not in conflict: how SES signs (the d= value) is separate from the alignment mode the recipient applies, which comes from your _dmarc record's adkim tag and defaults to relaxed when absent — as it is here. Net effect: both options pass today under p=none relaxed; Option B additionally survives a future move to adkim=s or p=quarantine/reject.

8 Setup — Option B (verify the subdomain)
  1. Create a domain identity for the subdomain
    In SES (region ap-northeast-1), create a Domain identity named stg-ex.backseat-service.com with Easy DKIM (RSA_2048). SES issues three DKIM tokens.
  2. Publish the three DKIM CNAMEs into the subdomain zone you control
    Add <token>._domainkey.stg-ex.backseat-service.com → <token>.dkim.amazonses.com (×3) to the stg-ex.backseat-service.com hosted zone in VerUp-STG (the zone whose NS are ns-604 · ns-58 · ns-1867 · ns-1250). No parent-zone access needed.
  3. Let SES detect & verify
    SES polls DNS automatically (usually minutes once propagated; up to a 72-hour window). Confirm read-only with aws sesv2 get-email-identity --email-identity stg-ex.backseat-service.com → expect DkimAttributes.Status = SUCCESS and VerificationStatus = SUCCESS.
  4. (Optional) Add a custom MAIL FROM for SPF alignment + bounce handling
    Set MAIL FROM to a different subdomain, e.g. bounce.stg-ex.backseat-service.com. Publish exactly one MX (feedback-smtp.ap-northeast-1.amazonses.com, priority 10) and the SES-provided SPF TXT. This makes mail pass DMARC via SPF as well as DKIM. Not required if DKIM alignment alone is acceptable.
  5. Clear the sending gates & codify
    Send From an address under stg-ex.backseat-service.com in ap-northeast-1; request production access if the account is still sandboxed; then declare the identity + DKIM in bs-exchange-infra to remove drift.
