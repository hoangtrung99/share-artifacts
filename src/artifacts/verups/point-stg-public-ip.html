05Risk & actions

RiskWhy "running low" matters

  1. 1
    az1 (1c) & az2 (1d) have only 7 free IPs — below AWS's recommended ≥8 free IPs/subnet for ALB auto-scaling. ALB point-alb runs in exactly these 2 AZs.
  2. 2
    Under load, the ALB adds nodes and needs free IPs in the subnet. If the subnet runs out → the ALB can't scale → risk of 5xx errors.
  3. 3
    Public subnets are /28 (11 IPs) — too small for a subnet hosting an internet-facing ALB. Private subnets are /26, secondary /24 — far larger.
  4. 4
    az4 (1a) has 10 free but the ALB isn't present there (2-AZ only) → IP load is imbalanced and that headroom is unused.

ActionsHow to relieve the /28

  • Remove the guardduty-data VPC Endpoint from the 2 public subnets (move to a private subnet — it already lives in the az4 private subnet). +1 free az1, +1 free az2 → 8/8, meets ALB threshold
  • Move EC2 point-stg-ec2-k8s to a private subnet + SSM (drop the public IP). +1 free az1 → 9 free
  • Long-term: resize public subnets /28 → /27 or /26 (requires recreating subnets — needs a maintenance window, part of the migration scope), or add az4 to the ALB to spread across 3 AZs.
  • Leave as-is: the 3 public NAT Gateways (1/AZ) are intentional multi-AZ HA.
Context
This is a subnet IP-exhaustion problem (private IPs count toward "Available"), distinct from EIP quota. Account-wide there are 8 EIPs in use (6 in VPC point + 2 in VPC cf-proxy-stg); Service Quotas reports the EIP limit as 5 yet 8 are actually allocated → the API figure is unreliable. Verify the real ceiling via EC2 Console → Limits before adding any resource that needs a public IP.
